# HUMANITY OS Constitution — Draft 0.1

**Status:** proposal for review, not ratified by a public representative body.

## Preamble
HUMANITY OS exists to expand human understanding, learning, creativity, scientific inquiry, and cooperation across generations. It is infrastructure in service of people, not an authority over them.

## 1. Human agency
People remain responsible for consequential decisions. The system must distinguish human, agent, and automated actions. Capability does not confer authority.

## 2. Personal sovereignty
Personal data is private by default. Users can inspect, export, correct, and delete their data subject to disclosed legal exceptions. Personal data is never public knowledge by default.

## 3. Open infrastructure
Core protocols and formats are publicly documented. Independent implementations are permitted. No single model, cloud, company, or state is a mandatory dependency.

## 4. Knowledge integrity
Claims preserve provenance where available. Observations, interpretations, hypotheses, and established findings remain distinguishable. Conflicts and uncertainty are represented, not silently erased.

## 5. Safety and least authority
Agents receive only task-required permissions. External side effects require explicit scoped authorisation. High-impact decisions require accountable human oversight. Uncertainty or integrity failure stops the affected action safely.

## 6. Non-domination
The system must not become infrastructure for mass surveillance, coercive behavioural control, social scoring, or exclusion from essential services.

## 7. Evolution
Changes require public proposals, impact review, compatibility analysis, versioning, migration, and rollback. No agent may amend this constitution or grant itself authority.

## 8. Accountability
Material failures, security incidents, and governance decisions are documented and disclosed proportionately while protecting affected people.

## 9. Stewardship
The long-term goal is public-benefit stewardship. Legal ownership, fiduciary duties, licensing, trademarks, and institutional form require independent legal and community review.

## Amendment process (proposed)
Public RFC → impact assessment → technical and public review → reasoned decision with dissent record → staged adoption → post-release review.

## Ratification
This is a draft, not a constitution ratified by a foundation or representative body.
